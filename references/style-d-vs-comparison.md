# Style D — VS Comparison

Reference image: `../examples/password-vault-storyboard-style-d-vs.png` ("Your Brain vs a Password App"). Study its structure: two fixed sides, two rival characters, judged rounds, and a scoreboard verdict in the last frame.

## Why this style (evidence)

In a 2025–26 analysis of 85,140 English Shorts, **"X vs Y" was the only title pattern with a clear, large effect — about 2× the median views** — and comparison channels scale on very few videos (Reigarw Comparisons: ~2.14M subscribers and ~795M views from ~107 videos). The format builds its own retention: the viewer stays to see who wins.

## Structure — the 4 beats become a match

| Beat | In VS form |
|---|---|
| 1 Shock | THE CLASH — both rivals face off, big VS badge, the stakes in the caption |
| 2 Cause | ROUND 1 — one dimension compared (memory, speed, safety…) |
| 3 Mechanism | ROUND 2 — the decisive dimension, where the loser's weakness is exposed |
| 4 Payoff | THE VERDICT — scoreboard (e.g. 3–0), winner with the trophy, caption states the score |

Add a third round inside Beat 3's scene time if the topic needs it; never exceed 3 rounds in a Short.

## Look

- Every frame split down the middle: **left side always BLUE, right side always ORANGE** (fix these side colors for the whole series).
- Each side has ONE rival character, drawn as a mascot with a face (a sweating brain, a smiling steel vault, a tired battery, a smug padlock…). The same two characters in every frame.
- Frame 1 carries a big circular **VS badge** in the center.
- The final frame shows a scoreboard with the numeric score and the winner holding a trophy, confetti on the winner's side.
- Bold flat cartoon, thick outlines, saturated colors, sports-broadcast energy.

## Honesty rule (important)

The verdict must be **fair**: the losing side keeps one real strength (say it in Round 1 — let it win that round or a point). A rigged match reads as an ad and kills trust; a fair fight that still ends 3–1 or 2–1 is more convincing than 3–0 every time. Facts follow `script-formula.md` discipline — every compared claim must be true.

## Title formula

`[Side A] vs [Side B]` — exactly the two rivals, nothing else needed. Examples: Your Brain vs a Password App · Public Wi-Fi vs Mobile Data · Free VPN vs Paid VPN · Android vs iPhone Security.

## Master prompt (storyboard)

```
One single storyboard image: a 2x2 grid of four vertical 9:16 phone-video frames in a bold versus-comparison cartoon style: every frame split down the middle, blue side on the left, orange side on the right, with a big VS badge in the center of frame 1. Very large bold caption bars at the bottom of each frame: black bar, white capital letters. Frame 1: [LEFT RIVAL] on the blue side, [RIGHT RIVAL] on the orange side, a huge VS circle between them, caption '[A] VS [B]'. Frame 2: [ROUND 1 visual — one dimension compared], caption '...'. Frame 3: [ROUND 2 visual — the decisive weakness exposed], caption '...'. Frame 4: a scoreboard showing [SCORE], the winning character raising a trophy, caption '[WINNER] WINS [SCORE]'. Punchy saturated colors, thick outlines, energetic sports-broadcast feel, no other text anywhere.
```

## Master prompt (single scene)

```
[SCENE VISUAL], bold versus-comparison cartoon style, the frame split down the middle with a blue left side and an orange right side, [LEFT RIVAL CHARACTER] on the left and [RIGHT RIVAL CHARACTER] on the right, punchy saturated colors, thick outlines, energetic sports-broadcast feel, vertical 9:16
```

## Rules

- Side colors and rival characters never swap sides mid-video or across the series.
- One dimension per round; the caption names the round's question ("WHO REMEMBERS 20 PASSWORDS?").
- The score in the final caption must match the rounds actually shown.
- Keep the two rivals likeable — the loser is outclassed, never humiliated.
